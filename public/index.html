    02 // What it does

    The 204 is already on the clipboard. fifi turns it into data you can sort, share and map.

    Read

    Handwriting included

    Photos, scans or PDFs. Crossed-out names, red-ink swaps and margin notes are read, not skipped.

    Structure

    A fixed ICS-204 schema

    Gemini fills the same JSON fields every time: header, operations personnel, resources, work assignment, comms, approvals.

    Organize

    One row per resource

    Each row carries the incident, period and division, so it still makes sense after you sort or filter.

    Flag

    Staffing at a glance

    Resources with one person are CRITICAL, none is NO_PERSONNEL. Counted per incident on the map.

    Comms

    Radio plan captured

    Function, channel, RX/TX frequencies and tones come out as their own rows for the comms unit.

    Map

    Matched to the live incident

    Extracted incidents are matched by name to the national feed and highlighted, with your form totals beside official ones.

    Export

    CSV for Excel & Sheets

    Built in your browser from one extraction, so nothing is processed twice. Formula-safe cells.

    Access

    Bring a key, or a link

    Use your own free Gemini key, or a private access link from the site owner. Keys are never stored on the server.

    04 // Inside the IAP

    The ICS-204 is where the Incident Action Plan meets the fireline.

    Each operational period, the planning section publishes an Incident Action Plan (IAP): a packet of standard ICS forms covering objectives, organization, assignments, communications, medical and safety.

    The ICS-204 Assignment List is the page crews actually carry. One per division or group, it lists who is assigned, how many people, where they're dropped off and picked up, the task, special instructions and the radio channels to use.

    That makes it the richest snapshot of staffing on the ground, and the hardest to aggregate, because it lives on paper and changes by hand. fifi starts there and is built to grow into the rest of the IAP.

    Know

    Google sees the form

    Reading the form is Gemini's job, so Google receives it. On free Gemini keys, Google may use content to improve its products. Paid keys are not used for training.

    Advice

    For confidential IAPs

    Use a paid Gemini key or your agency's own. Don't tick "Remember" on shared computers, and clear the map summary when you're done.

    Control

    Wipe it anytime

    Untick "Remember on this device" to forget your key. Press "Clear from this device" in the map panel to remove your incident summaries.

    One call returns structured JSON in the exact ICS-204 shape, handwriting included, with a free tier for public visitors and a stronger Pro model for invited users.

    07 // FAQ

    Questions

    Is fifi free?

    Yes. The site is free and open source. Extraction uses Google Gemini, so you either paste your own Gemini API key (Google AI Studio offers a free tier) or use a private access link from the site owner.

    Where does the map data come from?

    Incidents and perimeters come from the National Interagency Fire Center's WFIGS public feeds (current incidents). Hotspots are NASA FIRMS VIIRS detections from the NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 satellites over the trailing 24 hours. Satellite imagery is Esri World Imagery. Nothing on the map needs a key.

    What is a "hotspot", and is every hotspot a wildfire?

    A hotspot is a satellite pixel (about 375 m) that was unusually hot during an overpass. Fire radiative power (MW) is how intense it was. Most are vegetation fires, but some are prescribed burns, agricultural burning, flares or industrial heat. Use the confidence and power filters to cut noise.

    Are my forms or my API key stored?

    No. Each form is held in server memory only while Gemini reads it, then discarded; it is never written to disk or a database. Your key is used for that one request and never saved on the server. See Security & privacy for exactly where everything goes.

    How accurate is the extraction?

    Gemini reads typed and handwritten forms well, but smudged, faint or crossed-out entries can still be misread. Always review the CSV before using it for decisions.

    How does "matched to the live incident" work?

    fifi compares the incident name on your form with names in the national feed, ignoring case, punctuation and words like "Fire" or "Complex". If there's a match, the incident gets a gold ring and your form totals appear in its popup. Small or local incidents may not be in the national feed.

    Is this an official NWCG, FEMA or agency tool?

    No. fifi is an independent student project. For evacuations and official information, follow your local agencies and InciWeb.
